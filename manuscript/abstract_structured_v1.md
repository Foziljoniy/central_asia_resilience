# Structured abstract

## Background
Household shocks can worsen food insecurity, and remittances may help households smooth consumption.

## Methods
We estimate country-specific observational interaction models for Kyrgyzstan and Uzbekistan. Kazakhstan is benchmark context only.

## Results
Kyrgyzstan's interaction estimate was negative but imprecise (-0.214; 95% CI -0.655 to 0.227). Uzbekistan's broad-shock interaction was negative (-0.541; 95% CI -1.041 to -0.040), while household fixed effects attenuated the estimate (-0.177; 95% CI -0.551 to 0.197).

## Conclusions
The evidence is directionally consistent across countries but differs in precision and robustness. The study is observational, and L2CU estimates are unweighted.
