# Primary Research Question

## Working Paper Title

Do Remittances Buffer Household Shocks? Evidence on Food Insecurity in Kyrgyzstan and Uzbekistan

## Primary Question

Is the negative association between household shocks and food insecurity weaker among remittance-receiving households in Kyrgyzstan and Uzbekistan?

This is an observational association and moderation question. The project does not claim a causal impact unless a later credible identification strategy is developed.

## Core Mechanism

The empirical design requires, at minimum, a verified measure of remittance receipt, household shock exposure, and food insecurity or defensible welfare in each main country.

Revised Phase 2 verifies that minimum mechanism in both countries:

- Kyrgyzstan LiK: `h620` remittance receipt, household shock roster `shock`/`h701`, and adult food-insecurity items `i251_1`-`i251_8`.
- Uzbekistan L2CU: `mig_living_remittance` and `remittance_hh`, `work_lost_hh` plus major-change shock items, and FIES items `ln_1`-`ln_8`.

The resulting design decision is **FULL TWO-COUNTRY DESIGN**.

## Interpretation

Food-insecurity outcomes are expected to be coded so higher values mean worse insecurity. Under that convention, a negative remittance-by-shock interaction is consistent with a buffering association. If a later welfare outcome is coded with higher values meaning better welfare, the expected interaction sign reverses.
