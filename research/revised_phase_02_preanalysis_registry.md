# Revised Phase 2 Preanalysis Registry

## Design Freeze

The study proceeds as a **FULL TWO-COUNTRY DESIGN** using Kyrgyzstan LiK and Uzbekistan L2CU.

Paper title:
Do Remittances Buffer Household Shocks? Evidence on Food Insecurity in Kyrgyzstan and Uzbekistan

Research question:
Is the negative association between household shocks and food insecurity weaker among remittance-receiving households in Kyrgyzstan and Uzbekistan?

## Minimum Mechanism

Both main countries contain verified measures of:

- remittance receipt;
- household shock exposure;
- food insecurity or a defensible welfare outcome.

## Estimation Boundary

No model is estimated in Revised Phase 2. Later phases should construct country-specific datasets and estimate country-specific models only.

## Outcome Sign Convention

If the later food-insecurity score is coded so that higher values mean worse insecurity, the buffering hypothesis predicts a negative remittance-by-shock interaction. If a welfare outcome is used with higher values meaning better welfare, the expected sign is positive.

## Locked Caveats

- LiK remittance receipt is measured in a migration module and requires an explicit structural-zero rule.
- LiK food insecurity is adult-level; household aggregation, if used, must be pre-specified.
- L2CU food insecurity is available from round 49 onward.
- L2CU lacks verified region and rural/urban variables in the supplied CSVs.
- L2CU `popw` is not used until weight documentation is confirmed.
- Kazakhstan remains pending and outside the current empirical analysis.
